Projects

TAM-CNC app

TAM-CNC is an Android app that generates G-code for eight CNC milling operations. It uses the simplified style I have machined with for over a decade: clean subroutines, loops and interpolation that stay short, readable and easy to change.

You fill in the variables for the operation, such as depth, diameter or step-over, and tap Generate. Change one value, generate again, and you can see straight away how the code changes. For a quick one-off it is quicker than setting up CAM or conversational programming, and it is a good way to learn how real G-code is structured.

It began as a project to learn Python by recreating the logic I use at the machine, and it follows the same idea as input-driven programs I wrote for the VQC system on a Haas ST20Y about ten years ago.

The app is in open testing on Google Play. You can join with a Google account, with no other registration.

TAM-CNC teaching platform

A browser-based virtual CNC milling machine for schools and colleges. Students sign in with their institution's Microsoft account, write G-code and watch the toolpath, with nothing to install. It is in private preview.

I am also running a short survey for CNC operators and programmers as research for the platform. If you work with CNC machines, I would value your answers.

About

My background

I started as a machine operator in 1999 and moved into university engineering workshops in 2003. Since then I have made research parts on CNC mills, lathes and EDM machines, and taught students and staff how to use the equipment safely.

G-code is the part of the job I enjoy most. I write it by hand, I generate it from CAM, and I have now built tools that generate it from a short list of inputs. I am working on a way to teach all of it in a browser.

Bones Forge

Away from the machines I am a blacksmith and a general maker. Bones Forge is where that work lives: re-enactment clothing and kit, a custom electric guitar, and whatever I am building next.
